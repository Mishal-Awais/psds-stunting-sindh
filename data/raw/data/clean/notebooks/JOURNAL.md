## Initial Predictions
Before analysing the dataset, I formed the following predictions based on my understanding of the factors that may influence child stunting in Sindh. 
First, I predict that the stunting rate among children in the poorest wealth quintile will be at least 10 percentage points higher than among children in the richest quintile, because poorer households may face greater difficulties accessing nutritious food, healthcare, and adequate living conditions. 
Second, I predict that the stunting rate among children whose mothers have no formal education will be at least 5 percentage points higher than among children whose mothers have secondary education or above, because education may help mothers access health information and make informed decisions about child nutrition.
Third, I predict that the stunting rate in rural Sindh will be at least 5 percentage points higher than in urban Sindh, because healthcare, sanitation, and nutrition services may not be equally accessible across rural and urban areas.

However, I am less certain whether maternal education will remain associated with child stunting after accounting for household wealth. Since maternal education and household wealth may be related, I expect that accounting for wealth could change the observed relationship. I will examine whether the association changes, while recognising that this comparison alone cannot establish a causal effect.
These predictions are initial expectations, not established findings. I will compare them with the dataset and document whether the evidence supports or contradicts them.
